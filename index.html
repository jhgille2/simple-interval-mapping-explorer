Adjust marker density and population size and observe how the marker-derived QTL genotype probabilities — the weights of the genome scan — and the resulting LOD profile respond.

Scan summary

Statistical methodology

Model, weights, and scan statistic

For RIL i, let xi ∈ {−1, +1} be the unobserved QTL genotype (−1: Parent B homozygote, +1: Parent A homozygote) and yi the phenotype. The single-QTL model is

yi = μ + a xi + εi, εi ∼ N(0, σ²),

with a the additive effect (half the homozygote difference).

Genotypes are observed only at markers. In simple interval mapping the QTL genotype between markers is replaced by its conditional expectation given the nearest observed flanking markers (Haley–Knott regression). For a position λ between markers M1 (genotype g1) at recombination distance r1 and M2 (g2) at r2, with the RIL-by-selfing switch probabilities Rk = 2rk/(1 + 2rk) (Haldane–Waddington map expansion; Haldane map, no interference) and R12 = R1 + R2 − 2R1R2:

P(QTL = A | A,A) = (1−R1)(1−R2)/(1−R12)
P(QTL = A | A,B) = (1−R1)R2/R12
P(QTL = A | B,A) = R1(1−R2)/R12
P(QTL = A | B,B) = R1R2/R12

(Lander & Botstein 1989). At an observed marker the probability is 0 or 1; outside the observed marker range, or where both flanks are missing, it is the marginal 0.5. The scan regresses y on x̂i(λ) = 2Pi(λ) − 1 at each grid position; the plotted LOD compares this model against the intercept-only null. The genome-wide significance threshold is obtained by permuting phenotypes (Churchill & Doerge 1994).

Key assumptions: a single QTL on the chromosome, no crossover interference, RILs fully inbred by selfing, and residuals independent and normal. Haley–Knott regression is an approximation to full maximum-likelihood interval mapping; it is exact at markers and slightly conservative between them.

References: Lander ES, Botstein D (1989) Genetics 121:185–199; Haley CS, Knott SA (1992) Heredity 69:315–324; Churchill GA, Doerge RW (1994) Genetics 138:963–971; Broman KW, Sen Ś (2009) A Guide to QTL Mapping in Experimental Crosses, Springer.
